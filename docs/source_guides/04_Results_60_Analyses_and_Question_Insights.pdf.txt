VOLUME 4 / RESULTS AND LEARNING INTELLIGENCE
Emergent, implement this volume as the authoritative specification for scoring explanations,
results, question review, cohort comparisons and study recommendations. Deliver all 60
numbered analyses, with graceful unavailable states when evidence is insufficient. Build
working calculations and drill-through evidence before adding decorative charts. Connect this
engine to the exam adapters, approved question bank and immutable attempts specified in
the other volumes.
Use facts before interpretations
Provide an immediate score receipt after a submission has reached the server and the grading job
has completed. Identify the attempt, paper, exam preset, scoring version, key version and
submission reason. Show exact marks, full-correct, partial-credit, wrong, unanswered and excluded
counts. Separate awards caused by invalid questions from ordinary earned marks. Keep results
pending rather than fabricate a score while grading is unavailable.
Organize analysis into Score, Subjects, Topics, Time, Strategy, Questions, Cohort and Next Steps.
Every metric card must open its supporting question list. Each card must state its denominator,
evidence count, observation period and calculation version. A personal count can exist after one
attempt; an interpretation of ability requires broader evidence. Do not convert thin evidence into a
precise-looking strength score.
Deliver the complete review workflow
Let Tejas review every question with his final server-committed response, scoring contribution,
approved answer, source-linked solution, approved tags and time evidence. Display explanations
only after their release condition. Preserve the original question numbering and stable identifiers
even when options were shuffled. Make the same workflow available to hundreds of thousands of
learners through access-controlled, asynchronously generated reports.
Treat comparisons honestly
Label all platform comparisons as platform cohort comparisons. Never label a practice percentile
as an official NTA score, national rank, intelligence percentile or admission probability. Do not
estimate a question-wise ability percentile from a binary correct or wrong outcome. Instead
provide cohort response statistics and, where evidence supports them, a conditional
correct-response time percentile. Use the explicit cohort rules and suppression gates in this
volume.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Show a working report for every supported exam adapter.
Reconcile the score receipt and every drill-through total.
Leave unavailable metrics informative, with the missing evidence named.

One measurement contract and
reproducible score ledger
IMPLEMENTATION / DATA MODEL
Create a typed analytics contract shared by grading, dashboards, exports and admin tools. Do
not let chart components invent formulas or recalculate scores independently. Materialize a
versioned report from the immutable attempt and an explicitly identified approved scoring policy.
Reuse that report wherever the learner sees a result.
Store the calculation inputs
Store attempt_id, learner_id, paper_version_id, exam_adapter_version, scoring_policy_version,
answer_key_version, started_at, deadline_at, submitted_at and submission_reason. For each delivered
item store canonical_question_id, delivered_option_mapping, final_committed_response,
commitment_sequence, evaluation_outcome, signed_score_contribution, maximum_contribution and
award_reason. Keep browser-selected drafts distinct from server-committed responses. A draft shown
in the browser is not evidence that an answer was accepted.
Use ordinary eligible items E for skill and completion metrics. Partition evaluated nonempty responses
into full-correct C, partial-credit P and incorrect W, so attempted A = C + P + W. Represent
unanswered U separately. Exclude invalid or award-only items from these partitions and show their
count X. Define ordinary positive marks G, penalty magnitude L and separate award adjustment B, so
total S = G - L + B.
Make context explicit
Include approved subject, chapter, concept, prerequisite and editorial difficulty identifiers, along with
tag version and reviewer status. Include original first-exposure status, practice or timed mode,
accommodation preset, language, solution exposure and integrity review status. Never merge these
contexts silently. Multi-tagged questions may appear in several filtered views, but an overall count
must deduplicate canonical items.
Attach a metric object with metric_id, value, unit, numerator, denominator, sample_count,
eligibility_query_hash, evidence_state, unavailable_reason and calculation_version. Use null for
undefined values. Do not serialize Infinity, NaN or a zero that really means unknown. Store raw
precision and format only in the view layer; rounding must not affect grading. For sample medians,
average the two middle sorted values when n is even. For quartiles, use zero-based position h =
(n-1)*p and linearly interpolate its neighboring sorted values. Version this quantile method; keep
percentile ranks on the separate inclusive-count convention.
Revisions and reproducibility
Keep the historical report and create a new revision when an approved key, item status or scoring
policy changes. Explain every item-level delta and the total difference. Rebuild dependent cohort
statistics using the revised version, then publish the report and cohort snapshot atomically. A cache
containing an old key must not be mixed with a newly graded score.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Verify E = C + P + W + U for ordinary eligible items.
Verify S equals the sum of all item ledger contributions.
Expose report revision time and scoring policy identifiers.

CHAPTER 03 / 23
Percentiles, ties, cohort size and
comparability
MATHEMATICS / PLATFORM COHORT POLICY
Implement one documented platform percentile convention: P(s) = 100 * count(score <= s) /
N. Include the learner in the cohort when their attempt satisfies its eligibility rules. Give tied
scores identical percentiles. Use exact decimal or rational comparisons before display
rounding. This remains a platform statistic.
A testable numeric example
Use this ten-person demonstration cohort only in tests: scores 10, 20, 20, 30, 40, 40, 40, 50, 60
and 70. A score of 40 has seven scores at or below it, therefore platform percentile 70.00. A score
of 20 has percentile 30.00. The highest score has 100.00. Do not display this tiny fixture as a
production benchmark. The proposed production gate is at least 100 eligible distinct learners.
Specify who is being compared
Define a cohort by exact paper version, exam preset, effective key and scoring versions, mode,
timer policy, solution-release exposure state and a declared date window. Use each learner's first
valid attempt only when every ordinary eligible canonical item was unseen in recorded platform
history before start. An encounter in another paper, any hint, or prior solution access counts as
exposure and excludes the whole attempt from the primary paper cohort. Also enforce unexposed
eligibility for each item benchmark. Honor declared external exposure; unreported off-platform
exposure remains a limitation. Exclude unknown exposure histories; present exposed and repeat
attempts separately. Do not select each learner's best result: that biases the comparison upward.
Exclude staff preview accounts and confirmed invalid attempts; log every eligibility change.
Snapshot eligible N, exclusions, date window, update time and cohort query hash. Keep a running
cohort visibly provisional until its window closes. If N is below 100, show personal results and an
insufficient-cohort message. More learners do not repair a nonrepresentative sample; never
extrapolate platform results to all Indian examinees.
Separate official results and question statistics
Official JEE Main NTA percentiles use the relevant official shift population; the total percentile is not
the mean of subject percentiles, and percentile is different from percentage marks [S4-01]. Do not
recreate official normalization from this platform's participants. Do not merge different mock
papers into a national percentile.
For one binary-scored item show the proportion of eligible learners who answered fully correctly,
including unanswered learners in the denominator. Call this a cohort response statistic. A correct
learner belongs to a large tie group; that observation does not locate their individual ability. Keep
conditional time comparisons separate and exclude partial-credit items from binary-only claims.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Test ties, all-equal scores, negative scores and zero eligible learners.
Ensure total percentile is calculated directly from total scores.
Show N and cohort identity beside every comparison.

CHAPTER 04 / 23
Question review, approved solutions
and release controls
CONTENT / EXPLANATION UX
Build a complete question review workspace that preserves source fidelity and supports both
text and image-based questions. Students must understand the answer, their own response and
the scoring outcome without opening a separate PDF for every question. Approved source PDF
crops must remain available as a fidelity fallback.
The review panel contract
Display original question number, canonical identifier, subject and approved tags; the complete stem,
figures, passages and options; the learner's final committed response; accepted answer or answer
set; signed marks; and the grading explanation. Show partial credit, no credit, penalty, invalid item
and special award as distinct states. For numerical questions explain the configured accepted values
and precision rules rather than quietly rounding the student response.
Use the canonical option identity when explaining a shuffled option. If the learner saw option B but the
master PDF calls the same answer D, show both mappings clearly. Store original question PDF page
and bounding box, original solution PDF page and mapping evidence. Offer a report-content-problem
control attached to those identifiers, without changing the learner's saved response.
Make solutions trustworthy
Prefer the owner-provided approved solution. Permit an additional explanation only after subject
review; label its origin and version. Do not let an AI silently replace the supplied answer key, invent a
derivation or fix an ambiguous question. Offer step-by-step text, source images, accessible
mathematics and an optional alternate method. Keep a distinct correction notice when a supplied
solution has been reviewed and amended.
Use approved error notes for common distractors and numerical traps. Explain exactly which
assumption or step is wrong. A response alone seldom proves the student's reasoning, so phrase the
note as a possible misconception and let the learner confirm or reject it. Do not call every wrong
answer careless.
Enforce release on the server
Support immediate post-submission release, scheduled release after a test window, and
teacher-controlled release. Before release, return no answer key, hidden solution text, distractor
explanation or predictable asset URL in the API, page source, preload or export. Publish an explicit
release timestamp and show a pending explanation state. Snapshot solution exposure so later
attempts are correctly labeled as exposed practice.
Keep marks available when scoring is complete even if explanations are embargoed, subject to the
test owner's configured result-release policy. Never let cohort statistics leak a delayed answer
through an option marked correct.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Test embargoed APIs and PDF downloads using a student account.
Check every solution mapping against its canonical question.
Preserve source accessibility and zoom on small screens.

A01-A05: score and response
completeness
ANALYSIS CATALOG / 01 OF 12
Implement these five measurements from the authoritative item ledger. Permit exact
personal facts after one completed attempt, while keeping teaching interpretations cautious.
All counts refer to ordinary eligible items unless the card explicitly includes excluded or
award-only items. Let every count open a filtered question list.
A01 / Net awarded marks
Inputs: signed contributions under the current approved key. Formula: S = sum(score_q), including
explicitly identified awards. Evidence: a finalized grading revision; otherwise show grading
pending. Action: inspect the largest lost-mark contributors and any adjustment notice. Limit: raw
marks compare directly only within the same scoring context; they do not indicate national rank or
percentile.
A02 / Fully correct response count
Inputs: final committed response and full-correct evaluation outcome. Formula: C =
count(full_correct). Evidence: all relevant items graded; preserve a pending count if any remain
unresolved. Action: revisit correct answers whose reasoning felt uncertain. Limit: an observed
correct answer can result from guessing or prior exposure and does not establish concept mastery.
A03 / Incorrect and partial outcomes
Inputs: distinct evaluation outcomes. Formula: W = count(incorrect), P = count(partial_credit);
display both separately within this analysis. Evidence: approved adapter classification. Action:
open the wrong list first, then examine why partial answers failed full-credit criteria. Limit: a
partially correct multiselect response is not equivalent to a wrong single-choice answer; do not
flatten their explanations.
A04 / Attempted-answer accuracy
Inputs: C and A = C + P + W. Formula: 100 * C / A. Evidence: A > 0; show the exact fraction for any
A, but suppress strength labels below 10 attempts. Action: inspect inaccurate attempted topics
before increasing attempts blindly. Limit: excluding unanswered items can make selective
students look stronger; pair this value with completion and partial-credit counts.
A05 / Response completion rate
Inputs: attempted A and eligible delivered E. Formula: 100 * A / E, with unanswered count E - A.
Evidence: E > 0 and submission finalized. Action: distinguish unvisited questions from viewed but
unanswered questions before planning coverage. Limit: completion rewards submitting a response,
not correct reasoning; high completion can coexist with negative marks and poor accuracy.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Return null when A or E is zero.
Never count an unsaved browser draft as an attempt.
Keep excluded-item awards visible outside ordinary outcome counts.

A06-A10: marks earned, penalties and
subject balance
ANALYSIS CATALOG / 02 OF 12
Implement score decomposition without inventing a single strategy score. Read the signed
item contributions produced by each exam adapter; question categories may carry different
rewards, penalties and partial-credit rules. Present values in marks first and make all ratios
explicit about their denominator.
A06 / Ordinary positive marks earned
Inputs: ordinary item contributions s_q. Formula: G = sum(max(s_q, 0)), excluding separate
award-only adjustments B. Evidence: finalized ordinary grading. Action: identify which subjects and
question types generate the positive marks. Limit: G ignores penalties and is not the final result;
partial-credit contributions remain positive marks rather than full-correct counts.
A07 / Penalty marks lost
Inputs: negative ordinary contributions. Formula: L = sum(max(-s_q, 0)). Evidence: finalized
grading; zero is valid when the preset has no negative marking. Action: review the exact penalized
responses and their stated confidence where available. Limit: a penalty does not prove an
irresponsible guess; a student may have made a sincere reasoning error.
A08 / Penalty-to-positive-marks ratio
Inputs: L and G. Formula: 100 * L / G. Evidence: G > 0; otherwise show L with ratio unavailable.
Action: compare the ratio across comparable first attempts and inspect preventable error
categories. Limit: values may exceed 100 percent; do not clamp them. This is not penalty divided
by final net marks, which becomes unstable near zero.
A09 / Percentage of configured maximum marks
Inputs: total S and current effective maximum M from the scoring policy. Formula: 100 * S / M.
Evidence: M > 0 and all special awards resolved. Action: read alongside raw marks to understand
the scale of this paper. Limit: allow negative percentages where rules permit negative scores; this
percentage is not a cohort percentile or an official normalized score.
A10 / Subject marks contribution
Inputs: item-to-subject assignment and signed contributions. Formula: S_subject = sum(s_q within
subject); show G_subject, L_subject and awards separately. Evidence: every item has one
approved primary scoring subject. Action: choose a subject for detailed review based on its actual
mark losses and coverage. Limit: subjects with different item counts or time demands cannot be
judged solely by raw totals.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Reconcile subject sums exactly to the total score.
Test zero gross marks with nonzero penalties.
Display different marking categories rather than assuming one reward/penalty pair.

A13 / Response-type performance
Inputs: response type, signed marks and configured maxima. Formula: sum(s_q) / sum(max_q),

A16-A20: timer use and observable
question time
ANALYSIS CATALOG / 04 OF 12
Collect low-cost visibility and visit events, then distinguish official timer consumption from
observed on-screen activity. Reading can occur without interaction, and missing telemetry is
not inactivity. Define a reconciled timing ledger before deriving time advice. Suppress
question-time advice below 90 percent usable timing coverage for the relevant group.
A16 / Timer time consumed
Inputs: server start, effective deadline, authorized adjustments and submission receipt. Formula:
min(submitted_at, deadline_at) - started_at, adjusted by documented timer rules. Evidence:
complete server clock ledger. Action: examine whether substantial available time remained at
submission. Limit: elapsed time includes offline or hidden-page periods in an unpaused timed exam;
it does not measure active thinking.
A17 / Observed visible question time
Inputs: question-enter, question-leave, visibility and capped heartbeat intervals. Formula: union
duration of valid visible intervals attributed to each question, summed without overlap. Evidence:
valid timestamp order and coverage flags. Action: compare where observable attention was
allocated. Limit: visibility does not establish mental focus, and a static visible page may still be
actively read.
A18 / Unobserved timer fraction
Inputs: timer duration D, visible attributed T and recorded hidden duration H. Formula: 100 * max(D -
T - H, 0) / D after interval reconciliation. Evidence: D > 0; distinguish known hidden time from
missing events. Action: inspect connection or telemetry gaps before trusting timing advice. Limit:
unobserved time must not be called laziness, cheating or distraction.
A19 / Time on slow incorrect responses
Inputs: wrong items, observed time and approved per-item correct-response median. Formula:
sum(T_q where incorrect and T_q > 2 * benchmark_q). Evidence: five usable wrong items and at
least 30 correct cohort times per benchmark. Action: review stopping and revisiting tactics. Limit:
this descriptive threshold is configurable; slow errors may reflect legitimate effort, not wasted
thought.
A20 / Question budget overrun
Inputs: a student-approved pretest time budget b_q and observed T_q. Formula: sum(max(T_q - b_q,
0)); list individual overruns. Evidence: budget existed before the attempt and valid timing; otherwise
unavailable. Action: adjust the next practice budget after reviewing difficulty. Limit: the overrun is
measured against a plan, not an optimal schedule or guaranteed lost marks.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Do not double-count simultaneous tabs or duplicated heartbeats.
Test reconnection, backgrounding and early submission.
Keep the server deadline independent of the analytics heartbeat stream.

ANALYSIS CATALOG / 05 OF 12
Implement these analyses from ordered visits and accepted response commitments. A

A21 / Subject time allocation
Inputs: valid question-visible time and primary subject. Formula: 100 * T_subject / sum(T_subject),

A22 / First-visit duration
Inputs: each eligible item's first valid visible visit. Formula: median(first_visit_seconds), with a
distribution by outcome. Evidence: at least five usable first visits per reported group. Action: identify
where an initial scan became an extended attempt. Limit: first-visit time may include reading shared
passages or diagrams; it is not pure solution time.
A23 / Revisit time share
Inputs: visible visits ordered per item. Formula: 100 * sum(duration of visits after the first) / total
attributed time. Evidence: positive total and at least five visited items. Action: inspect whether
revisits resolved uncertainty or repeated an unproductive approach. Limit: a high revisit share can be
a sensible checking strategy; it is not automatically inefficient.
A24 / Positive score difference after revisions
Inputs: first committed response, final response and current scoring policy. Formula:
sum(max(score(final_q) - score(first_commit_q), 0)). Evidence: complete commitments for revised

changes. Limit: this compares recorded endpoints and cannot prove that changing answers caused
improvement or predict future gains.
A25 / Negative score difference after revisions
Inputs: the same committed endpoints. Formula: sum(max(score(first_commit_q) - score(final_q), 0)).
Evidence: complete revision history; annotate clears and timeout acceptance rules. Action: study
harmful changes and the evidence used to justify them. Limit: final correctness is hindsight; do not
prescribe never changing answers from one outcome or count uncommitted drafts as lost marks.

A29 / Time spent on viewed omissions

A30 / Median time on fully correct items

Question-wise risks, precautions and
expected-value reasoning
INTERPRETATION / EXPLAINABLE STRATEGY
Generate each question's risk and precaution notes from approved content plus recorded
evidence. Separate a content trap, an input-format trap and a decision tradeoff. Give the
learner a practical next check. Never replace a reviewed explanation with a generic warning
or imply that a probability model knows what the student truly understood.
Use a disciplined note template
For every flagged question show: observation, supporting evidence, possible explanation, next
precaution and limitation. Example: "Your final response has the wrong sign. The solution changes
sign when the integration limits are reversed. Check the limit order before simplifying. This
response alone cannot prove where your reasoning diverged." Attach the exact solution step or
reviewed content note. Let the learner label their cause after reading.
Support reviewed traps such as units, domain restrictions, extraneous roots, sign conventions,
numerical formatting, option quantifiers and multiselect completeness. Display only notes relevant
to the approved item and its adapter. A blank response gets a coverage or decision note, not an
invented misconception. Correct but slow items may receive an alternate-method suggestion;
correct but low-confidence items may receive a reasoning check.
Explain the model rather than prescribing guesses
For a simple +4/-1 item, EV = 4p - (1-p) = 5p - 1, so the arithmetic break-even probability is 0.20. A
hypothetical 0.25 probability gives EV 0.25 marks per attempt. A +1/0 policy gives EV p when
blank scores zero. These are examples of configured rules, not assumptions that all exams use
them.
Explain that p is generally unknown. Elimination of options improves a theoretical probability only
if the eliminated options really cannot be correct. Time has an opportunity cost, and the remaining
test may contain better uses of that time. Multiselect, partial-credit, bonus and numerical-response
rules need their own outcome models; disable the binary calculator when those assumptions fail.
Offer simulation as post-test reflection
Permit students to try a transparent hindsight scenario, such as removing selected low-confidence
penalized answers. List changed items and recompute with the exact adapter. Label the result
"hypothetical score on this attempt." Do not call it a guaranteed attainable score. A hindsight
scenario must not alter the saved attempt, published cohort or official-format result.

Bound the meaning of time evidence

and a modest capped heartbeat. Use monotonic client intervals and server-receipt validation; reconcile

Quantify uncertainty without pretending representativeness
For binary cohort proportions, display counts and a Wilson 95 percent interval when the independence
approximation is reasonable. With p = k/n and z = 1.96, center = (p + z^2/(2n))/(1 + z^2/n); half-width

repeated exposure, shared coaching or correlated items. Do not show this formula as an ability interval
for a single learner. Do not manufacture a percentile confidence band from arbitrary point adjustments.

guarantees.
