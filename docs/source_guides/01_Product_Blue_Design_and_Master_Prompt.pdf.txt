Build Tejas Exam Studio, a polished blue-themed exam practice platform for Tejas and a large

Copy-ready master instruction

Product promise, boundaries, and
honest claims
PRODUCT CONTRACT
The product succeeds when a student can complete a dependable practice attempt and
understand what to improve next without needing help to interpret the interface.
The promise to the student
Offer faithful practice for supported exam profiles, clear custom practice for selected chapters,
readable questions and solutions, and analysis that identifies concrete actions. Preserve the
distinction between an exam simulation and a teaching session. A simulation follows its approved
navigation and submission rules; a learning session may reveal hints or explanations only if
explicitly enabled. Every attempt shows its mode before starting and in its final report.
Treat Tejas as an initial student persona, not as a special privileged account. His restricted chapter
selection and integer-only practice requirements are supported through general configuration that
any permitted student can use. Keep personal student names out of public marketing copy and
shared examples unless they are intentional, approved content.
Boundaries that prevent misleading behavior
Do not promise official exam scores, admission chances, guaranteed ranks, or psychological
diagnoses. Do not call a self-selected practice cohort the national population. Never produce a
percentile when the metric definition lacks a suitable cohort, eligibility rules, or minimum evidence.
Provide an unavailable state with a practical explanation. Predicted outcomes, when supported
later, must have a model version, uncertainty, validation, and a clearly visible prediction label.
Question quality and answer correctness are editorial responsibilities. AI may suggest extraction,
tags, or explanations, but an attractive interface does not validate those outputs. Show content
quality status to administrators and prevent unapproved content from appearing in student
attempts.
Scope for the first working release
Deliver approved exam profiles, PDF import review, a reliable exam shell, scoring, question-wise
review, basic progress history, and a meaningful subset of the Volume 4 analytics. Build the
remaining analytics as available data permits. The required launch threshold is at least 40 correctly
implemented, distinct analyses with explicit unavailable states; 60 is the complete specification.
Repeated visualizations of the same number do not count as separate analyses.
Acceptance language for Emergent
Copy this requirement: Every visible product claim must correspond to a tested capability or a
clearly stated limitation. Use actual application records for screenshots and demos. Label
demonstration data. Produce a short release note that states what works, which exam profile
versions were verified, and which planned capabilities remain unavailable.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
The landing page says independent practice platform and names supported profiles accurately.
Results distinguish measured values, derived values, unavailable analyses, and predictions.

Modes, personas, and role boundaries
ONE PRODUCT, EXPLICIT MODES
Use a small set of understandable modes and roles. Avoid granting privileges because a user
reaches an administrator-looking URL.
Student modes
Full Simulation loads an approved complete paper, its exam profile, its time budget, and its allowed
interaction rules. For WBJEE, this is a digital practice adaptation; never market it as an official CBT
replica. Custom Practice lets a student select approved subjects, chapters, question types, count, and a
supported time setting. Label integer-only JEE-style practice as Custom Practice when its composition
differs from the official profile. Topic Learning is an optional later mode with hints and immediate
explanations. Its attempts must be excluded from simulation comparison cohorts unless a metric
specifically includes them.
Each attempt stores mode, paper revision, exam-profile version, content release, language,
accommodation setting, and cohort eligibility. These identifiers stay fixed after start. A saved attempt
cannot acquire different scoring rules because an administrator updates the profile while it is in progress.
Personas and the tasks they need
A first-time student needs a quick orientation, one suitable paper, and confidence that answers save. A
returning student needs unfinished attempts, previous errors, and a small next-action list. A parent or
mentor may need an explicitly shared progress view, but sharing is optional and permission-controlled. A
content editor needs PDF comparison, unresolved import issues, question editing, and validation. An
exam-rule reviewer needs evidence and profile approval. An operator needs incident visibility and
support tools with limited access.
Role design
Define Student, Content Editor, Content Reviewer, Exam Profile Reviewer, Support Operator, and
Platform Administrator as permission sets. Small teams may assign multiple sets to a person, but each
privileged action still requires its relevant permission. Separate uploading from publication approval by
default. Support personnel cannot browse complete student answer histories merely because they can
resolve a login ticket.
Invite and remove administrator access through an auditable interface. Require additional authentication
for sensitive actions as specified in Volume 5. Never expose role changes, answer keys, or unpublished
solutions to public clients.
Mode selection interaction
Present a simple choice with a one-sentence explanation and clear consequence. A simulation card
states the profile and separate result and solution release conditions, including any scheduled embargo.
A custom-practice card says that chapter and composition settings differ from a full exam. Show a
confirmation if changing a configured practice mode would discard selections.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
A student token cannot publish a paper, read unreleased solutions, or assign roles.
Reports and comparison filters always show or retain the attempt mode.

The restrained blue design system
EXACT VISUAL TOKENS
Build a consistent interface from tokens. The exam workspace should feel calm, legible, and
predictable for a long attempt.
Color tokens and their purpose
Use primary #1D4ED8, primary-hover #1E40AF, primary-soft #EFF6FF, page-background
#F5F8FD, surface #FFFFFF, text-strong #0F172A, text-muted #475569, border #CBD5E1, success
#166534, success-soft #F0FDF4, warning #92400E, warning-soft #FFFBEB, danger #B91C1C, and
danger-soft #FEF2F2. Use focus-ring #2563EB with a visible outer offset. Verify every actual
text/background pairing and state rather than assuming a token alone passes accessibility.
Status must combine color with a label, symbol, or shape. Marked-for-review, answered, and
unanswered states must remain distinguishable in grayscale. Avoid gradients behind mathematical
content, low-contrast pastel text, pulsing urgency effects, and decorative backgrounds in the exam
area.
Typography and spacing
Use Inter when available, with system-ui and sans-serif fallbacks. Use a separate tested math
renderer and its compatible fonts. Desktop body text is 16 px with 1.5 line height; question content
defaults to 18 px with 1.55 line height and offers a readable size setting. Small metadata is 14 px.
Headings use 24, 30, or 36 px only where hierarchy warrants it. Avoid condensing body text to fit a
card.
Use spacing steps 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, and 64 px. Cards have 12 px corner radius; controls
have 8 px radius; dialogs have 16 px radius. Default card padding is 24 px on desktop and 16 px on
narrow screens. Use light borders and restrained shadows. The question canvas is the visual
center.
Layout tokens
Set reading content to a comfortable maximum width of 720 px, general dashboard content to
1200 px, and the desktop exam palette to roughly 280 px where space permits. Treat these as
layout targets that must adapt without clipping. Use a consistent header, breadcrumb, page title,
and action placement. Avoid displaying more than one competing primary action in the same
decision area.
Implementation direction
Define the tokens centrally as CSS variables and application theme values. Components consume
tokens rather than hard-coded ad hoc colors. Ship light mode first; dark mode is optional only after
question figures, math, status colors, contrast, and screenshots have all been verified.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Check the complete theme against WCAG 2.2 contrast and focus expectations [V1-S1].
At 200% zoom, controls remain visible and no question or answer is clipped.

CHAPTER 05 / 27
Components and predictable interaction
UI CONTRACT
A component is complete only when its loading, focus, disabled, error, and success states are
understandable.
The shared component inventory
Create buttons, text fields, selects, numeric answer fields, radio options, checkboxes, tabs, dialogs,
tooltips, notices, skeletons, badges, empty states, tables, pagination, paper cards, question cards,
palette cells, save indicators, and metric cards. Document permitted variants and accessibility
semantics. Keep student and administrator interfaces visually related while allowing denser editorial
tools.
Buttons must have an action label: Start simulation, Continue attempt, Save and next, Review paper,
or Publish approved paper. Avoid generic Submit wherever it hides what will happen. Show a loading
label only when a request is actually running. Prevent duplicate actions through the mutation
contract, with interface feedback that does not depend solely on disabling a button.
Interaction details
A modal dialog announces its purpose, receives focus, traps focus while open, and returns focus to its
trigger when dismissed. Keep destructive actions visually distinct and require an intentional choice for
the affected record. Menus close predictably with Escape. Tooltip content is supplemental;
instructions required to complete an action must be visible without hovering.
For editable fields, associate labels, guidance, and validation errors programmatically. Preserve
entered values after a failed request. Set focus to the first relevant error when a form cannot proceed,
without destroying the rest of the input. Keep inline feedback close to the control; use a summary
when there are several failures.
Exam-specific exceptions
Use stable option positions throughout an attempt. Do not animate selected options, shuffle answers
after a revisit, or show correctness before the mode permits it. A numeric field must reflect the
allowed answer syntax of the approved profile; it must not infer permissibility from a browser
keyboard alone. Keyboard shortcuts are discoverable and configurable, and must not intercept text
entry.
Copy-ready implementation requirement
Build a component gallery route accessible only to the team. Show every interaction state with actual
semantics, not screenshots. Include a long mathematical stem, a tall figure, mixed-language content,
a large option, a failed save, and a narrow viewport. Reuse these fixtures in device checks. Document
any deliberate differences between the official simulation controls and ordinary dashboard controls.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Keyboard users can activate, exit, and revisit every interactive component.
Disabled controls explain their condition when the user needs that explanation; success messages
reflect server-confirmed outcomes.

Routes, navigation, and permission
guards
SITEMAP THAT IMPLEMENTS THE PRODUCT
Create clear destinations and preserve context through navigation. A route list is not
sufficient without data access and state behavior.
Student routes
Use / for the public landing page; /sign-in and /onboarding for access and orientation; /dashboard
for the student home; /exams for the catalog; /exams/:paperId for details; /practice/new for custom
configuration; /attempts/:attemptId/instructions for readiness; /attempts/:attemptId for the exam
shell; /attempts/:attemptId/results for the overview; and /attempts/:attemptId/review/:questionId
for question-wise review.
Use /analytics for longitudinal views, /library for bookmarked and previously missed questions,
/planner for next actions, /profile for preferences and permitted data controls, and /help for product
support. Keep URLs stable enough that a student can reopen a permitted result link. A shared link
must apply its own authorization policy and never inherit accidental access from a guessed
identifier.
Administrator routes
Provide /admin/papers, /admin/imports/:importId, /admin/questions/:questionId, /admin/profiles,
/admin/reviews, /admin/cohorts, /admin/support, and /admin/audit with role-specific guards. Route
guards improve navigation but server authorization controls every record and action. Editors do
not gain access to other organizations or all student records by changing a URL.
Navigation behavior
The student sidebar groups Home, Exams, Results, Question Library, and Planner. Hide optional
unfinished areas. Keep the active destination visible and retain catalog filters when returning from
exam details. The exam shell uses its own focused layout; ordinary marketing navigation must not
distract from an active attempt. Navigating away invokes only the warnings and continuation
behavior permitted by that profile.
Handle not-found, unavailable, forbidden, and signed-out states separately. If an expired session
can be renewed, preserve the intended safe destination. Do not put sensitive answer values,
private student identifiers, or access tokens into URLs.
Deep-link acceptance
Copy this direction: Test each protected route as an owner, another student, a signed-out visitor,
and each relevant administrator role. Verify the API denies forbidden data before it reaches the
client. Opening an already-submitted attempt redirects to its permitted report; opening an
unavailable paper gives an explanatory page rather than a broken exam shell.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Browser Back restores useful catalog or review context without restarting an attempt.
A forged URL cannot reveal another student report or unpublished answer key.

Onboarding, sign-in, and a useful first
session
REDUCE FRICTION WITHOUT HIDING CHOICES
A new student should understand the platform and reach a suitable paper quickly. Collect only
information with a clear product purpose.
The onboarding sequence
Start with account access, then ask for a display name, target exams, preferred interface language,
and an optional preparation stage. Explain why each field is useful. Keep examination year
selectable rather than inferring it from age or school year. Allow students to skip optional
personalization and change it later. Do not request marks, school, phone number, or demographic
details simply to make onboarding look complete.
Offer two first actions: Try the short interface rehearsal or Browse papers. The rehearsal uses
demonstration questions, records no competitive percentile, and clearly says it is not an official
mock. Returning students see Continue attempt only when a continuable attempt exists under its
profile rules.
Account behavior
Use the authentication mechanisms implemented and verified in Volume 5. Provide clear request,
confirmation, expired-link, retry, and signed-out states. Avoid revealing whether a particular email
belongs to a student through uncontrolled account lookup. Keep sign-in feedback consistent and
specific enough to help the person using it. Support deliberate sign-out and explain what happens to
a running attempt before the student takes that action.
Treat a student account as personal. If mentor views are included later, the student initiates a
scoped sharing invitation, sees what will be shared, and can revoke it. No public profile or
leaderboard participation is assumed at registration.
Preferences and responsible defaults
Store readable question size, language, motion preference, notification choices, and permitted
accessibility preferences. Provide an explicit first-session privacy notice with links to the applicable
policy. Handling minors, consent, and data retention requires the applicable implementation review
described in Volume 5; do not invent an age threshold or call a checkbox legally sufficient.
Default to a quiet dashboard with no autoplay, no notification barrage, and no pressure countdown
unrelated to an exam. Do not turn the first login into a mandatory purchase or a multi-screen survey.
First-session quality
Present the chosen exam, a suggested available paper, its mode, and its expected time
commitment. If suitable approved content is unavailable, say so and show alternatives without
silently switching examination formats. Show a help link near the first start action.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
A new account can start a suitable available paper without completing optional fields.
Refreshing onboarding, signing out, and returning do not create duplicate accounts or discard valid
preferences.

Catalog, search, and paper discovery
HELP STUDENTS PICK THE RIGHT ATTEMPT
Make the exam catalog informative enough to choose a paper without exposing unreleased
questions or solutions.
Catalog structure
Provide tabs or filters for exam family, profile year, Full Simulation or Custom Practice, subject,
chapter coverage, duration, language, difficulty descriptor, and availability. Display only meaningful
filters backed by indexed approved data. Use an obvious Clear filters action and preserve selections
in safe URL parameters. Search matches paper titles and approved topic labels; it does not leak
private solution text.
Each paper card displays title, examination family, profile version or year, mode, subject coverage,
question count, permitted duration summary, content quality status, and the action available to this
student. A published card can say Reviewed. Avoid a decorative Verified badge unless the exact
verification scope is explained.
Selecting and comparing papers
A paper detail link is separate from Start. Show completed-attempt count and a clearly labeled latest
personal score where useful. A student who already started the same assigned attempt sees
Continue, not another ambiguous Start button. An optional Compare view may show up to three
papers using comparable attributes; it must not imply their raw marks are directly comparable
across examination families.
Display difficulty labels as editorial or empirical according to their source. Empirical labels need their
cohort and version; new papers can have an editorial label or show Not yet calibrated. Do not
fabricate the number of students who took a paper.
Empty and unavailable states
When filters match nothing, retain the filters, explain the mismatch, and offer to remove the most
restrictive selection. When the catalog is empty, say No published papers are available yet. If access
is required, explain the applicable entitlement accurately. If an import is still under review, hide it
from students or label it as unavailable without offering Start.
Search loading must not resemble an empty result. A failed request shows Retry while preserving the
query. Keep prior results visibly stale only if that state is clear and safe.
Implementation direction
Copy this requirement: Build catalog search with stable pagination or a tested incremental-loading
strategy, keyboard-operable filters, and understandable result counts. Test a large paper list with
long names and many chapters. Ensure the backend applies publication and access filters before
returning records, rather than filtering restricted records only in the browser.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
A student can identify exam family, mode, and time commitment before starting.
No unpublished questions or solutions are included in catalog responses.

Exam details and pre-start readiness
MAKE THE COMMITMENT UNDERSTANDABLE
The exam detail page should answer what the student will take, under which rules, and what is
needed for a reliable attempt.
Information before the start action
Show the paper title, independent practice disclaimer, exam family, year-specific profile, mode,
language, subject and section structure, question count, duration summary, scoring summary, and
publication revision. All numerical rules come from Volume 2 and the approved configuration. Link
to the expanded instructions. Custom-practice details show selected chapters and deviations from a
full simulation prominently.
Explain whether the student can pause, resume after disconnection, move across sections, revisit
answers, and change answers after advancing. Use the actual approved profile behavior. Avoid
universal phrases such as Resume anytime when only some modes permit it. State when solutions
become available and which events end the attempt.
Readiness check
Check whether the browser can load the question assets and communicate with the attempt
service. Offer a short demonstration of selecting an option, entering a numeric answer, marking
review, and moving between permitted questions. Show recommended device conditions as
guidance; do not require a desktop if the product supports phones. Keep the readiness check
separate from a guarantee that the connection will never fail.
Show current connectivity and a tested save-status example. If prerequisites fail, describe the
failing requirement and provide a retry path. Do not allow a failed readiness check to consume an
examination timer unintentionally. Accommodations must be selected and authorized before the
attempt snapshots its profile.
Starting without duplicate attempts
The start button creates or resumes the appropriate attempt through an idempotent server action.
Show Creating attempt and move to instructions only after confirmation. A double click, refresh, or
repeated network request must not create duplicate assigned attempts. If a practice policy
intentionally allows another attempt, label it Start another attempt and describe any comparison
consequences.
Version and fairness disclosure
Store the displayed paper revision and rule version with the attempt. If the paper becomes
unavailable before start, show a clear explanation and return to discovery. If a published correction
affects completed attempts, use the revision and regrade procedure in Volumes 3 and 5; do not
quietly replace the report that the student previously saw.

The shown rules match the configuration that the server snapshots at start.
A failed start request preserves selections and offers a safe retry without duplicate records.

Instructions and the interface rehearsal
CONFIRM UNDERSTANDING, NOT JUST A CHECKBOX
Use concise instructions with expandable detail. Important consequences must remain visible
before the timer begins.
Instruction page composition
Begin with the mode, examination profile, and duration. Present answer types and scoring,
navigation restrictions, save behavior, review markers, timing, submission, and permitted
continuation as separate readable blocks. Include an accessible legend for palette statuses.
Numerical and transition details are supplied by Volume 2. Show a short section summary even
when the full rule text is available below.
Place the primary Begin timed attempt action at the bottom of the required instruction content, with
the time consequence beside it. Do not preselect a rule-acceptance control. Agreement records
acknowledgment of these instructions; it is not a substitute for an actual working save and timer
system. If rules have materially changed since a previous attempt, highlight the change.
Rehearsal behavior
Offer a separate untimed rehearsal using obvious demonstration content. Practice choosing an
option, clearing a permitted answer, entering a numeric value, marking review, opening the palette,
and confirming a demonstration submission. The rehearsal must reflect the interaction affordances
of the selected profile, while avoiding false claims that it reproduces every official
examination-room condition.
After rehearsal, return to instructions without carrying answers into the real attempt. Explain any
control that differs by mode, such as hints being available in learning practice but not simulation.
The student may skip rehearsal after understanding the controls.
Accessibility and clarity
Provide a textual status legend, visible focus, readable tables, and an accessible order for screen
readers. Do not express review status using colors alone. Keep rule sentences short. Explain
ambiguous terms such as attempted, submitted, and marked for review using the approved state
definitions. Provide a translated interface where approved, without automatically translating
mathematical question content.
Instruction acceptance evidence
Copy this acceptance request into Emergent: Demonstrate instructions for each supported
examination family and for custom integer practice. Show that starting begins the correct timer only
after the server transition succeeds. Test back navigation, refresh, delayed network response,
double activation, and session expiry. No request failure may leave the student believing the exam
started when the server has not created an active attempt.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
A student can explain when an answer is saved and when an attempt becomes final.
The rehearsal does not contaminate analytics, rankings, or actual attempt responses.

The desktop examination workspace
FOCUS ON THE QUESTION
Implement the exam shell as a dedicated workspace with stable controls and no unnecessary

Layout and reading
Use a compact top bar for paper identity, the approved timer display, connection and save status,

Render the full question stem, options, tables, formulas, units, and figure captions. Preserve

Answer interactions
For single-choice questions, support exactly the selection behavior of the configured type.

permitted. Never represent review status as a substitute for a response value. The transition engine

celebratory animation appear during a simulation. Do not shift the content vertically when a save
succeeds. Keep the question scroll position stable while updating background indicators. Refresh

color.

Question navigation and status
semantics
THE PALETTE IS A VIEW OF STATE
Students must be able to locate unfinished work without interpreting hidden status rules or losing a
response during navigation.
Palette model
Use the approved profile to determine which statuses exist and how they map to saved responses and
review markers. A common product display may distinguish unseen, seen without response, answered,
marked for review, and answered plus marked for review when those states are permitted. The
authoritative mappings, transitions, and scoring consequences come from Volume 2; do not assume this
display vocabulary applies unchanged to every exam.
Every palette cell has a visible question number, an accessible label containing position and status, a
focus state, and a distinct current-question indicator. Include a persistent legend or an easily reachable
legend control. For many questions, use a scrollable palette that keeps the current section clear and does
not trap keyboard users.
Moving between questions
Changing questions follows the configured navigation policy in Volume 2. Distinguish unsaved drafts from
responses committed by an explicit SAVE or REVIEW action permitted by the profile. If navigation discards
a draft under that policy, explain the consequence in instructions and the unsaved indicator; preserve the
committed answer. Background draft recovery is never an exam commitment. Handle pending commits
under Volume 5.
Where section access is restricted, unavailable destinations explain the reason. A profile may allow
returning to a question, forbid certain backtracking, or impose section timing. Implement those behaviors
from the configuration rather than disabling controls cosmetically. Student-visible instructions and server
enforcement must agree.
Keyboard and visibility
Provide predictable keyboard access to the palette, answer controls, and navigation actions. Announce
question changes without repeatedly reading the entire dashboard. Do not steal focus when a background
save completes. On narrow screens the palette opens as a drawer with an accessible close action and
returns focus to its trigger.
Show section-level counts only from current authoritative state. If an event is still awaiting
acknowledgment, separate its pending indicator from the confirmed count. Do not round or estimate the
number of answered questions.
Verification direction
Copy this test requirement: Exercise every allowed transition for each profile, including selecting then
clearing, answering then marking review, viewing without answering, revisiting a saved numeric answer,
and attempting a forbidden transition. Compare the resulting palette, saved response record, and scored
result against the same state-transition specification.

A readable first screen
Start with marks and subject splits, then show attempted, correct, incorrect and skipped counts.
Put a short explanation beside the platform cohort percentile, including eligible N and the snapshot
date. If no valid cohort exists, show an unavailable state instead of a fabricated comparison. Use a
compact tab layout for overview, question review, timing and study plan.

The question review table must link the committed response, award, measured timing, verified
tags and released solution. Selecting a row opens the full question and explanation, preserving the
result filters. Include a small, evidence-linked plan, then make all 60 analyses searchable by
category. Keep formulas, coverage limits and revision lineage available on demand.

Shared contract across all five volumes
CANONICAL NAMES | API PREFIX | RULE PRECEDENCE
Treat the five volumes as one specification. Resolve wording differences through the
following shared contract before implementing APIs, migrations or scoring. Record any
deliberate deviation in the project decision log.
Canonical entities and naming aliases
Use Question (also called Item) for the reusable bank identity and QuestionVersion (ItemVersion)
for an immutable content snapshot. ExamConfig or ExamProfile describes the versioned rules; an
Adapter is the code that interprets those rules. PaperVersion is the published ordered manifest of
question versions, configuration version, solution versions and release policy. Attempt binds one
student to that immutable manifest. GradingVersion records the scoring implementation, key
revision and correction lineage. Internal naming may differ, but every migration and API must map
these concepts consistently.
One API contract and two response states
Use /v1 as the canonical API prefix and publish one OpenAPI specification. Short /admin, /attempts
or /uploads routes in worked examples are endpoint families under /v1, rather than separate
unversioned APIs. Separate draft_response from committed_response. Background persistence
may recover a draft, but only a profile-eligible explicit commitment event changes the response
eligible for scoring. Show local edit, durable draft save and committed-answer acknowledgement
as distinct states. Finalization freezes the eligible committed responses; it must not invent a
missing Save or Review action.
Precedence and change control
A current official rule source establishes the relevant exam baseline. The administrator-approved
immutable exam configuration implements that source. Volume 2 defines its executable
interpretation; Volume 3 binds reviewed content to it; Volume 4 derives analysis from accepted
attempts; Volume 5 preserves those contracts in production. Volume 1 controls product language
and presentation. Custom practice overrides must be labeled and versioned. If a verified source
conflicts with a proposal, correct the proposal, publish a new approved version and demonstrate
regression fixtures. Do not change rules for attempts already running.
ACCEPTANCE / PRECAUTIONS
Generate typed clients from the canonical OpenAPI contract and validate all example endpoint
families against it.
Prove that a durably autosaved draft remains unscored until the required commitment action.
Before launch, publish a traceability matrix linking requirement, configuration, implementation
and test evidence.
